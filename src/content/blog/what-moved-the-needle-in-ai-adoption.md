I help drive AI adoption for my department at Lenovo, which sounds like a role about models and prompts. In practice it's a role about Tuesday afternoons — the hours people lose to ticket triage, spec hunting, and writing the same status update in four places. The AI work only landed once I optimized for the afternoon, not for the AI.

## What didn't work

- **A separate AI destination.** Another chat window people had to remember to open was dead on arrival. The tools that survived lived *inside* Jira and Confluence, where the work already was.
- **Impressing people.** Demos got applause; applause didn't change workflows. Usage came from tools that saved someone visible time on a real task.
- **Mandates.**Nothing kills curiosity like requiring it.

## What did work

1. **Meet the workflow where it is.** Skills and hooks inside the tools people already lived in — the Atlassian extension with agents and MCP servers underneath.
2. **Ship small, measure loudly.** Every early skill did one chore end-to-end. When time-lost dropped, we said the number out enough times that people came asking.
3. **Local models where it counts.** Ollama for anything sensitive made security reviews shorter and adoption faster — the boring choice that unblocked everything else.
4. **Teach the humans.** Training sessions and enablement did as much for adoption as any feature.

## The number

The extension and everything around it cut overall time lost across the department by 65%. That's the entire argument I have for the approach — and I've learned it's the only argument that matters. Nobody adopts AI. They adopt faster Tuesdays.
